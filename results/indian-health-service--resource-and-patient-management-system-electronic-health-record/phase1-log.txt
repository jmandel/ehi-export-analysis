Patient Care Component (PCC)\n\nThe **Patient Care Component (PCC)** is the clinical heart of RPMS -- its central clinical data repository.\n\n### Origins and Design\n- Designed by **Clayton Curtis, Don Enos, and Bill Mason** at the Tucson development center between 1983-1985\n- Built to hold legacy PCIS (Patient Care Information System) data from the predecessor mainframe system\n- First implemented at Tucson area sites around 1986\n\n### Architecture: The V-File Structure\nPCC introduced a revolutionary data structure -- the **VISIT file with associated \"V\" files**:\n- **V MEASUREMENT** (vitals, measurements)\n- **V PROVIDER** (providers involved in visits)\n- **V POV** (purpose of visit / diagnoses)\n- **V LAB** (laboratory results)\n- **V MICROBIOLOGY**\n- **V BLOOD BANK**\n- **V HOSPITALIZATION**\n- And many more visit-related files\n\nThis was described as \"the first expression of a relational idiom to data stored in FileMan.\" George Timson (FileMan creator) added forward and backward relational navigation to FileMan specifically to accommodate this design. Who Uses RPMS\n\nRPMS serves three categories of users, known as the **I/T/U** community:\n\n1. **IHS Federal Facilities** -- Hospitals and clinics directly operated by the Indian Health Service\n2. **Tribal Health Programs** -- Tribes operating their own health programs under the Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act (P.L. 93-638) and Tribal Self-Governance Act. These \"Compacted/Contracted\" tribes may choose to use RPMS or adopt commercial alternatives.\n3. **Urban Indian Health Organizations** -- Urban Indian health programs serving AI/AN populations in cities\n\n**Scale:**\n- IHS serves approximately **2.6 million** members of **574 federally recognized tribes** across **12 service areas**\n- The network includes **45 hospitals and more than 600 other facilities** (clinics, health stations, etc.)\n- A 1993 GAO report documented **229 operational RPMS sites**; deployment continued through the 2000s\n- A study referenced **2,000 RPMS users** across I/T/U sites (likely a sample; actual user count is much higher)\n- 93% of surveyed users agree that RPMS needs replacement\n\n**Notable non-IHS users:**\n- Community Health Clinics of West Virginia (later transitioned to Athena Health)\n- Most Hawaii public health sites\n- Select American Guam clinics\n\n**Tribal sites that have left RPMS for commercial alternatives:**\n- Alaska Native Medical Center (ANMC) -- switched to Cerner in the mid-2000s\n- Multiple California tribal sites -- commonly using NextGen\n- Various other tribal programs exploring Epic, NextGen, athenahealth, and other commercial options\n\nSources:\n- [Harmony HIT - New EHR After 40 Years](https://www.harmonyhit.com/new-ehr-after-40-years-of-rpms-signals-legacy-data-overhaul-for-indian-health-services/)\n- [History of RPMS](https://smh101.com/articles/Hx_RPMS_final.html)\n- [IHS Find Healthcare](https://www.ihs.gov/findhealthcare/)\n\n---\n\n## 4. Architecture\n\n### Database and Language\n- **Programming Language**: MUMPS (M) -- both a programming language and database that originated in the 1960s for healthcare\n- **Database Platform**: Currently runs on **InterSystems Ensemble** which includes the **Cache database** (now transitioning to **InterSystems IRIS**). The BMW infrastructure package references \"HealthShare IRIS 2022\" upgrades.\n- **Data Management Layer**: **VA FileMan** serves as the database management system, providing semantic metadata (the data dictionary), data integrity, and validation\n- **Application Infrastructure**: **VA Kernel** provides the runtime environment ensuring seamless transactions between the operating system and application code\n\n### Client-Server Architecture\n- **Server**: RPMS database runs on servers (historically IBM RISC mini-computers with AIX/Unix; now Windows Server and IBM AIX)\n- **Client**: The EHR GUI (VueCentric) runs on Windows client computers\n- **Communication**: Remote Procedure Call (RPC) Broker handles client-server data exchange\n- **Framework**: **VueCentric** (owned by Medsphere Systems Corporation, acquired from Clinical Informatics Associates in 2006) provides the graphical presentation layer. It loads COM (ActiveX) objects as components into a central framework.\n- **Design**: Component-based architecture allows updates to GUI components without full application reinstallation. No dedicated EHR server is required; the RPMS server hosts data and a centralized object library.\n\n### Decentralized Model\n- RPMS runs on **regional/local installations** at individual facilities, as opposed to a single central cloud server\n- Each facility operates independently while remaining connected to the broader Indian Health system network\n- This creates **data silos** -- a significant limitation identified in modernization assessments\n\n### Hardware Evolution\n- **1984-1990**: PDP-11 clones (Plessy Peripheral, DEC clones), Burrows mini-computers with dumb terminals at 300-500 baud\n- **Early 1990s**: IBM RISC mini-computers with Unix operating systems\n- **Current**: Cache/IRIS on MS Windows Server and IBM AIX\n\nSources:\n- [IHS EHR Technical Overview](https://www.ihs.gov/ehr/technicaloverview/)\n- [RPMS and VistA Convergence](https://smh101.com/articles/rpms_vista_convergence.html)\n- [History of RPMS](https://smh101.com/articles/Hx_RPMS_final.html)\n\n---\n\n## 5. Relationship to VistA\n\nRPMS and VistA (Veterans Health Information Systems and Technology Architecture) share a common ancestor in the VA's **DHCP (Decentralized Hospital Computer Program)**.\n\n### Shared Components\nBoth systems use identical foundational infrastructure:\n- **Kernel, FileMan, and MailMan** packages\n- Same **Lexicon and code sets**\n- **National Drug Data** from the VA\n- **BCMA** (Bar Code Medication Administration), **VistA Imaging**\n- ICD/CPT codes\n\n### Key Differences\n| Aspect | RPMS | VistA |\n|---|---|---|\n| **Original focus** | Outpatient/ambulatory care | Inpatient/hospital care |\n| **Population** | AI/AN communities; diverse age groups | Veterans; predominantly male, older |\n| **Clinical Repository** | Patient Care Component (PCC) with V-files | Patient Care Encounter (PCE) -- derived from PCC but heavily rewritten |\n| **Billing** | Full billing, accounts receivable, third-party billing | Minimal billing (VA is government-funded) |\n| **Population Health** | Strong GPRA reporting, iCare, Q-Man query tools | Different approach |\n| **GUI** | VueCentric (Medsphere) | CPRS (Computerized Patient Record System) |\n\n### Code Exchange History\n- IHS adopted VistA infrastructure packages starting in 1983\n- Most RPMS clinical packages (Pharmacy, Lab, Radiology) originated from VistA\n- The VA adopted RPMS innovations: the **Patient Care Component became Patient Care Encounter (PCE)**, and the **Problem List** was imported from RPMS\n- However, the VA \"heavily modified\" RPMS code upon import, creating incompatibilities\n\n### Convergence Difficulty\nA detailed analysis shows varying difficulty levels for convergence:\n- **Easy**: Inpatient Pharmacy (6 of 218 routines modified), Consults (1 of 153 modified), BCMA, VistA Imaging\n- **Moderate/Hard**: Outpatient Pharmacy (111 of 430 routines modified + 176 extensions), Lab (819 of 1,132 routines modified + 197 extensions), Scheduling, TIU\n- **Extremely Difficult**: PCC vs PCE represents the most significant barrier -- fundamentally different clinical philosophies and data storage approaches\n\nSources:\n- [RPMS and VistA: Can They Be Converged?](https://smh101.com/articles/rpms_vista_convergence.html)\n- [History of RPMS](https://smh101.com/articles/Hx_RPMS_final.html)\n\n---\n\n## 6. Patient Care Component (PCC)\n\nThe **Patient Care Component (PCC)** is the clinical heart of RPMS -- its central clinical data repository.\n\n### Origins and Design\n- Designed by **Clayton Curtis, Don Enos, and Bill Mason** at the Tucson development center between 1983-1985\n- Built to hold legacy PCIS (Patient Care Information System) data from the predecessor mainframe system\n- First implemented at Tucson area sites around 1986\n\n### Architecture: The V-File Structure\nPCC introduced a revolutionary data structure -- the **VISIT file with associated \"V\" files**:\n- **V MEASUREMENT** (vitals, measurements)\n- **V PROVIDER** (providers involved in visits)\n- **V POV** (purpose of visit / diagnoses)\n- **V LAB** (laboratory results)\n- **V MICROBIOLOGY**\n- **V BLOOD BANK**\n- **V HOSPITALIZATION**\n- And many more visit-related files\n\nThis was described as \"the first expression of a relational idiom to data stored in FileMan.\" George Timson (FileMan creator) added forward and backward relational navigation to FileMan specifically to accommodate this design. Remarkably, **\"once the V File structure was developed, it hasn't changed\"** -- the fundamental structure has been stable since the mid-1980s.\n\n### What PCC Does\n- Serves as an **electronic abstract of the health record**\n- Acts as a **permanent repository for patient data**\n- **Integrates data from multiple facilities** and multiple data systems\n- Receives data automatically from other RPMS modules (Lab results, Pharmacy data, etc.)\n- Stores all commonly used English language terms encoded into ICD codes\n- Supports comprehensive patient health summaries (PCC Health Summary)\n- Enables population health queries through Q-Man, VGEN/PGEN, and iCare\n\n### PCC vs. VA's PCE\nPCC was adopted by the VA as **Patient Care Encounter (PCE)** but was heavily rewritten:\n- **PCC** stores ALL visit-related data in visit files (labs, meds, hospitalizations), uses IHS-specific visit classifications, aggregates visits together, and assumes coders review every encounter\n- **PCE** divides visits into separate sub-visits, does not incorporate coder workflows, and uses different data storage approaches\n- This divergence represents the single most significant barrier to RPMS/VistA convergence\n\n### Significance\nPCC's structured visit-file architecture enables capabilities that \"to this day cannot be easily done in VistA\" -- specifically, straightforward patient data retrieval for population health queries, and the ability to consolidate services into unified bills.\n\nSources:\n- [History of RPMS](https://smh101.com/articles/Hx_RPMS_final.html)\n- [RPMS and VistA Convergence](https://smh101.com/articles/rpms_vista_convergence.html)\n- [PCC Training Materials](https://www.ihs.gov/rpms/training/course-materials/?p=rpmsTraining%5CPCC-DataEntryI%5C04PPCCBriefing.pdf)\n\n---\n\n## 8. Modernization and Replacement Plans\n\n### The Problem\n- A year-long assessment by HHS and the **Regenstrief Institute** found:\n  - **60.3%** of RPMS EHR users believe the system needs \"significant improvements or a complete overhaul\"\n  - **30.3%** rated overall quality as \"poor\" or \"very poor\"\n  - **40%** rated electronic sharing of information with patients as \"very poor\" or \"somewhat poor\"\n  - **93%** of users agree now is the time to replace RPMS\n- Technical assessment concluded RPMS \"will be completely unsupportable in 10 years\" under current configuration\n- The VA's decision to move to a commercial EHR makes maintaining shared VistA infrastructure non-sustainable\n\n### Funding Disparities\n- The VA received **$1.6 billion** (with total costs exceeding $10 billion) to transition to Cerner\n- IHS received only a proposed **$25 million** for FY2020 -- despite serving a population 27% the size of VA's\n- **Prior to 2020, no dedicated budget existed** for IHS health IT modernization\n\n### The Decision\n- In **November 2023**, IHS selected **General Dynamics Information Technology, Inc. (GDIT)** to build, configure, and maintain a new enterprise EHR using **Oracle Cerner technology**\n- The contract has a potential value of **$2.5 billion over 10 years**\n- Life-cycle cost estimate: **$4.5 to $6.2 billion**\n- FY2024 budget request included $913 million; planned $1.1 billion annually for FY2025-FY2029\n- **Mitchell Thornbrugh** (enrolled member of the Muscogee Creek Nation), CIO and Director of the Office of Information Technology, oversees the effort\n\n### Timeline\n| Phase | Period |\n|---|---|\n| Research and planning | 2018-2021 |\n| Procurement and configuration | 2022-2024 |\n| Training, deployment, operations | 2025+ |\n| Pilot sites launch | Planned for 2025 |\n\n### Data Migration Strategy\n- Only **recent clinical data (24 months)** will migrate to the new EHR\n- A **\"Four Directions Warehouse\"** -- a central repository in federal cloud -- will store comprehensive RPMS data archives\n- Legacy data must comply with Federal Records Management: medical records retained 3-7 years after last patient episode; inactive records transferred to Federal Records Centers after 3 years, maintained for 75 years\n- Systems affected include VistA Imaging, iCare, Laboratory Services, Clinical Reporting System, Electronic Dental Record\n- Officials identified data migration as the primary concern: ensuring \"a smooth data migration, aiming to prevent data issues such as data loss, misinterpretation, or mis-mapping fields\"\n\n### Tribal Choice\n- Tribes recognized as \"Compacted Shares\" may choose to implement their own commercial EHR **or** adopt the enterprise system chosen by IHS\n- This is significant because tribal sites have always had the option not to run RPMS\n\nSources:\n- [HCI Innovation Group -
