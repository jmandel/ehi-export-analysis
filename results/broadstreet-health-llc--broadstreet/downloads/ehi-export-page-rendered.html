EHI Export

BroadStreet Health exports EHI in formats defined by the CDA 2.1 and FHIR R4 standards. BroadStreet Health ensures that our exported electronic health information is both comprehensive and interoperable, facilitating seamless data sharing across the healthcare spectrum.

Clinical Document Architecture (CDA) 2.1

Overview: CDA, or Clinical Document Architecture, is a standard developed by Health Level Seven International (HL7) to ensure uniformity in the structure of electronic clinical documents. CDA 2.1 represents one of the more recent versions of this standard and is widely used in health IT systems.

Key Features of CDA 2.1:

  • Structured Documents: CDA ensures that all clinical documents have a consistent structure, including a header and a body.
  • Human Readability: Every CDA document is human-readable, meaning that in its most basic form, it can be displayed and understood by clinicians.
  • Machine Processability: Beyond the human-readable component, additional structured data can be added, making the document machine-processable.

Structure:

  • Header: Contains metadata about the document itself such as the patient, author, and type of document.
  • Body: Houses the clinical information and is divided into sections. Each section can have one or multiple entries detailing the clinical act, such as a diagnosis, observation, or medication.

Usage in BroadStreet Health: Our system exports patient data in the form of CDA documents that adhere to the CDA 2.1 standard. These documents can be seamlessly shared and integrated into other EHR systems or healthcare IT systems supporting the CDA standard.

For a more in-depth understanding and the specification of CDA 2.1, please refer to the official HL7 CDA documentation.

Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR) R4

Overview: FHIR (pronounced “fire”) is another standard developed by HL7, focusing on the interoperability of healthcare information. Unlike the document-centric approach of CDA, FHIR is resource-oriented, with each resource representing a different type of clinical information.

Key Features of FHIR R4:

  • Granular Data: Instead of whole documents, FHIR deals with individual pieces of clinical information, like a patient's demographics, an observation, or a medication order.
  • Web Standards: FHIR is built upon modern web standards, making it easier for developers to work with.
  • Extensibility: FHIR resources can be extended or adapted for specific use-cases or to support local workflows.

Resources: FHIR defines a set of resources for different clinical concepts. Amongst other, it includes resources, such as:

  • Patient: Information about an individual receiving care.
  • Observation: Measurements or simple assertions made about a patient.
  • Medication: Details about a medication that can be prescribed.
  • Practitioner: An individual with a formal responsibility in the healthcare process. Examples include doctors, nurses, and pharmacists.
  • Encounter: An interaction between a patient and the healthcare provider, such as a visit or an inpatient admission.
  • Procedure: A clinical activity or intervention performed on or for a patient, like surgery or a diagnostic test.
  • Condition: A clinical condition, problem, or diagnosis about a patient.
  • Immunization: A record of an immunization that has been given to a patient.
  • AllergyIntolerance: Details about an adverse reaction or allergy a patient has to substances, including medications.
  • MedicationRequest: A request for a medication to be administered or dispensed to a patient.
  • CarePlan: A plan or protocol established to manage a patient's specific health concerns.
  • Device: Details about a medical device that is used on or for a patient, such as a pacemaker or an insulin pump.
  • DiagnosticReport: The findings and interpretation of diagnostic tests performed on patients.
  • Appointment: A scheduled interaction between a patient and a healthcare provider.
  • Organization: Represents an organization, such as a hospital or clinic, involved in the care of a patient.

Usage in BroadStreet Health: When exporting data in FHIR format, our system provides a bundle of FHIR resources. Each resource within the bundle contains a segment of the patient's health data. This granular approach ensures that data can be easily integrated, searched, and combined with data from other sources.

For more comprehensive details and the specification of FHIR R4, please refer to the official HL7 FHIR R4 documentation.

Broadstreet Notes (HTML)

Overview Notes created in Broadstreet can be exported in HTML format with the following structure.

Physician Information

  • Physician Name: The name of the physician who provided the service.
  • Sent by: The individual who sent the documentation.
  • Date: The timestamp when the documentation was sent.

Visit and Patient Details

  • Date of Service: The date when the service was provided.
  • Type: The type of service provided (e.g., H&P for History and Physical).
  • Patient Name: The full name of the patient.
  • Date of Birth: The birthdate of the patient.
  • Gender: The gender of the patient.
  • Advance Directive Code: Any code related to advance directives for the patient.

Medical Concerns

  • Allergies: Any known allergies that the patient has.
  • Primary Concern: The main reason for the patient's visit.

Social History

  • Smoking Status: The patient's smoking habits.

Treatment Review

  • This section would typically include past and ongoing treatments.

Vital Examination

  • Blood Pressure: Systolic/Diastolic blood pressure measurement.
  • Pulse: Heart rate measured in beats per minute.
  • Weight: Patient's weight.

Assessment Plan

  • Diagnosis Code: The ICD code for the diagnosis.
  • Assessment: The physician's findings and recommendations.

Visit Details

  • Next Appointment: Details of the next scheduled appointment including the date and physician.
  • Reason for Next Visit: The purpose for the upcoming visit.

Additional Notes

  • Any additional remarks from the physician regarding the patient's health or treatment advice.

Signature

  • Electronically Signed By: Confirmation of who has signed the document electronically.
